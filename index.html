What it does

Built for people who collect documents from many web pages — researchers, public-records archivists, internal-portal users, designers, students, and anyone tired of clicking "Save as…" hundreds of times.

📑
Combine pages into HTML

Turn related web pages into one readable HTML.

Find and review pages

Use automatic discovery for a quick start, or detailed controls for menus, paths, sitemaps, and pasted URL lists. Review page titles and remove or reorder anything before collection.

Save one portable document

Embed images for offline reading or keep online references for a smaller file. Linked PDFs, Office files, and other documents can be saved alongside the HTML.

🔎

Finds files others miss

708 file types (extensions) supported. Detects iframe-embedded PDFs, lazy-loaded images, fetch/XHR file responses, blob URLs, CMS download routes, hidden form values, and 39 data-* attributes that plain link scrapers ignore. Also reaches into Shadow DOM / Web Components, SPA hydration data (Next.js, JSON-LD), Cache Storage / OPFS / IndexedDB, and signed cloud-storage URLs (Cloudflare R2, Firebase, Supabase, Vercel Blob).

⚙️

Smart filtering

Filter results by extension, file size, file name, page title, or modification date. Sort, manually reorder, and copy URLs as plain text.

🕸️

Same-site crawl

Choose a depth (0–5 or unlimited) and visit same-site links within the starting page's folder (start at the site root to cover the whole site) to surface PDFs on subpages, viewer URLs, and CMS document routes — up to 100,000 pages per run. It also reads sitemap.xml / robots.txt and follows JavaScript-menu (javascript:/onclick), frameset, image-map, and GET search/pagination links, reaching pages that have no plain HTML links.

📚

Local PDF merge

Combine selected PDFs into a single file with one click. No upload, no server. Your files stay on your device.

🗜️

ZIP archive

Bundle selected files into a single ZIP for tidy distribution, archiving, or sharing — generated entirely in your browser.

📊

CSV export

Export URLs, file names, sizes, types, and titles to CSV for documentation, audit, inventory, or downstream automation.

🔐

Scans pages you can access

Uses your current browser session to scan intranets, LMS portals, and member-only pages you are already authorized to open. It does not collect credentials or override access controls.

🛡️

Works on complex pages

Handles JavaScript-rendered SPAs (React, Next, Nuxt, Vue), viewer routes, and CSRF-protected forms when they are part of pages you are authorized to access. It confirms file types by signature, not just extension, and can scan legacy HTTP pages.

⚙️

Built for big jobs

Scan many URLs at once or crawl an entire site, then control parallel downloads, pause/resume, auto-retry failures, sort into folders by type or domain, and rename files from their PDF title.

When to use it

How Grab All Files fits with other tools

Grab All Files focuses on two jobs: finding files on complex or login-protected pages, and turning selected pages into one readable HTML. Download managers and site copiers solve different parts of the workflow.

🔎

Find files on complex or login pages

PDFs, images, and documents hidden behind iframes, viewers, JavaScript, logins, and CMS routes. Grab All Files detects them, lets you filter and select, then bulk-downloads, merges PDFs, builds ZIPs, and exports CSV, all locally.

🚚

Download many known URLs or huge files

For very large files or long, unattended batch jobs with queue management and resume, a dedicated download manager (JDownloader, aria2) suits that better. Grab All Files finds the files and exports the URL list, which you can hand to the download manager.

🗺️

Need an exact offline copy of a whole website?

Use a website copier such as HTTrack when you need a navigable replica that preserves the original pages, links, and structure. Grab All Files instead combines the readable content of selected pages into one HTML with a table of contents; it is not a pixel-perfect site mirror.

FAQ

What file types can I download?

Grab All Files supports 708 file types (extensions), including PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, images, ZIP, and CSV. It also detects split / multi-volume archive sequences (.000/.001…, WinRAR .r00/.s00, WinZip .z01) and groups them with the other compressed archives. Filter the results by extension, select what you need, and download them all in one click.

Is it free to use?

Yes. Free bulk-downloads up to 10 files per download and finds related pages so you can choose exactly one page to save as HTML. Images on that chosen page have no plan-based count cap; its directly linked files are saved 10 at a time, and “Save the next 10” on the result screen saves the rest. Safety and source-site limits still apply. Pro is a one-time $19.99 purchase that removes the file cap and combines the main content of several selected pages into one HTML.

What happens when I reach the 10-file limit?

Nothing is locked. Free saves up to 10 files per download. When that download finishes, select the next files in the list and click “Save selected files” again — repeat until you have everything. Pro removes the per-download cap.

Can I combine multiple web pages into one HTML file?

Yes, with Pro. Free finds and lists related pages and saves the one page you choose. Pro lets you select several of them and combines their main content into one searchable, printable HTML with a shared table of contents.

How much does Pro cost?

$19.99 USD as a one-time purchase. Lifetime license, no subscription. Activates on up to 3 devices; a device is one browser profile on one computer, and you can free a slot by removing a device from the license screen inside the extension.

How do I activate Pro after buying?

Your license key is shown on the confirmation page right after payment and is also sent to you by e-mail. Open the extension, click “Free” at the top right (or open the ⋯ menu and choose “Pro & license key”), paste the key and click “Activate”. When the label at the top right changes to “Pro”, you’re done.

Does it send my browsing data anywhere?

No. Files are downloaded directly from the source site to your device. Only license verification and optional pseudonymous usage statistics reach our servers. Usage statistics are off by default and sent only after explicit opt-in; each event carries a random installation ID, the extension version, your plan and coarse counts or labels (for example, how many files were found). None of these include page content, URLs, or file names. See Security & permissions for details.

Which browsers are supported?

Google Chrome™ and Microsoft Edge™ (Chromium 111 or later), and Mozilla Firefox™ (128 or later).

Can I get a refund?

As a digital product delivered immediately, refunds are generally not available. The Free plan lets you confirm compatibility and try the one-page HTML and 10-file workflows before buying Pro. If a technical defect prevents the extension from working, contact us within 30 days of purchase for a full refund.

Does it work on protected sites that require a login?

Yes. The extension uses your existing browser session to scan pages you are already authorized to access, such as intranets, LMS portals, and member-only areas. It does not collect credentials or override access controls.

How do I download all files from a web page at once?

Install Grab All Files for Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, or Mozilla Firefox, open the page (or paste its URL), and the extension lists every detectable file, including files hidden behind iframes, viewers, lazy loading, fetch/XHR responses, blob URLs, and CMS download routes. Select the files you want and download them all in one click.

How can I download all PDFs from a website?

Grab All Files finds PDFs on a page, including iframe-embedded PDFs and PDF viewer URLs, and can crawl same-site links up to 100,000 pages per run. Filter the results to PDF, select all, then bulk-download them or merge them into one PDF locally in your browser.

What is the best Chrome extension to bulk-download files?

Grab All Files is a bulk file downloader for Google Chrome, Microsoft Edge, and Mozilla Firefox. It finds downloadable PDFs, Office documents, images, ZIPs, and CSVs that ordinary link scrapers miss, then saves selected files in one click, merges PDFs, builds ZIP archives, and exports a CSV of file URLs. Free saves up to 10 files per download; the $19.99 one-time Pro license removes that per-download limit.

How do I save all images from a web page?

Grab All Files detects images including lazy-loaded images, CSS background images, and blob URLs. Filter the results to image types, select them, and download them all at once — or export the list of image URLs as CSV.

Can I merge PDFs without uploading them to a server?

Yes. Grab All Files merges selected PDFs locally inside your browser. Nothing is uploaded, and your files never leave your device.

Can it handle large or long-running downloads, with pause and resume?

Yes. Grab All Files has a built-in download manager with adjustable parallelism, pause and resume, automatic retry, and one-click retry of failed files, so large batches keep going even if a connection drops. For very large single files or long unattended jobs, you can also export the URL list and hand it to a dedicated download manager.

Can I use it together with a download manager like JDownloader or aria2?

Yes, they complement each other. Grab All Files is best at finding files on complex or login-protected pages (behind iframes, viewers, JavaScript, and CMS routes). Export the results as a CSV or URL list and feed them to your download manager for the batch download itself.

Does it work on JavaScript apps, login-only pages, and bot-protected sites?

Yes. It reads JavaScript-rendered single-page apps (React, Next.js, Nuxt, Vue) after the page builds, uses your existing browser session for pages you are already authorized to open, follows CSRF-protected download forms, and detects Cloudflare/WAF bot-walls to guide recovery. It confirms file types by signature, not just by extension, and never bypasses access controls or security protections.

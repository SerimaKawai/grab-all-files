✓Before the deadline

A site is about to close or be rebuilt. Here is what to save first.

When a site has a date after which it will change or disappear, the problem is not how to save pages but which ones to save before time runs out. This guide orders the work: pages that exist nowhere else, then the documents they link to, then the look and structure, then the rest. It also lists what cannot be saved, so there are fewer surprises near the deadline.

Three things the saved copy keeps

What you can still save

  • ✓Pages as the browser displays them, including pages built by JavaScript and pages behind your sign-in.
  • ✓PDF, Word, Excel and PowerPoint files linked from pages.
  • ✓Video and audio files that a page links to directly, such as mp4 or mp3.
  • ✓Failed files: retry them, or export the list of failed URLs with the reasons.

What cannot be saved

  • –Streamed video, such as HLS or DASH streams and players that use blob: addresses.
  • –Search boxes and forms that ask the server. The saved pages do not connect to the network.
  • –Pages you cannot open in your own browser, or that need a sign-in you do not have.
  • –Files in DRM or encrypted viewers, and text drawn only on a canvas.

Checklist for the last week

  • ✓Write down the start pages: the home page, the document library, the news archive, the contact page.
  • ✓Sign in first if part of the site is for members. If the session expires during a long run, the run pauses; sign in again and press Resume. Pages already saved stay.
  • ✓Count the linked documents early. Linked files are saved 10 per run on Free and 100 on Pro, with a button on the result screen for the rest, so a library of hundreds of files takes several rounds.
  • ✓Open the saved copy with the network off. Click through a few pages and check that the links between saved pages open the saved files.
  • ✓Keep the date. With “Keep the original look” and “HTML with contents and search”, the source URL and capture time appear under each page’s title.

When the site has thousands of pages

Grab All Files reads pages inside your browser, one after another. That is how it reaches pages behind your sign-in, and it is also why a very large site takes long. With thousands of pages and little time left, a desktop website copier, which asks the server for pages directly, covers more ground per hour. Use Grab All Files for the pages that program cannot see, and for pulling out the linked documents by type.

Perguntas frequentes

What should I save first from a site that is closing?

Pages that exist only there, such as notices, prices and procedures, and the PDF and Office files they link to. The look and the old archive pages come after.

Can I browse the saved site offline by clicking links?

Yes, with “Site copy in a folder” on Chrome or Edge, or “Separate HTML pages (ZIP)”. Links between saved pages open the saved files. Links to pages you did not save still point to the live site.

Are PDFs and Word files saved too?

Yes. Files linked from the pages can be saved with the pages (10 per run on Free, 100 on Pro), or collected with Bulk File Download. Their text is not merged into the HTML; the page keeps the link.

Can it save video from the site?

Only video files linked directly from a page, such as mp4. Streamed video is not saved, and no DRM or watermark is bypassed.

The site has thousands of pages. What should I use?

A desktop website copier, which asks the server for pages directly and is faster for large public sites. Use Grab All Files for sign-in pages and for pulling out the documents.

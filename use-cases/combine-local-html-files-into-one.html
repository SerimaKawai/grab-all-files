Combine a folder of HTML files into one HTML file.

Have a saved website, an exported manual or a ZIP full of .html pages? Choose the folder or ZIP on your computer and Grab All Files combines the pages into a single HTML file with a table of contents. Pages keep the order of the site’s own links, local images are embedded, and links between pages jump inside the file. Everything runs in your browser; nothing is uploaded.

Workflow

  1. 1Open Grab All Files, choose “Combine pages into HTML”, and switch the source to “From a computer folder”.
  2. 2Choose the folder or a ZIP file, or drag it onto the page. .html, .htm and .xhtml files are listed in the order the site links them from index.html; unlinked pages follow by name.
  3. 3Review the list: keep or remove pages, switch to file-name order, limit to a sub-folder or leave out paths. Pick a purpose: Keep the site, Share as one file, Print or make a PDF, or Give it to an AI.
  4. 4Create the HTML. Check the report for files that could not be read, links to pages you left out and images that could not be embedded.

FAQ

Can I try this for free?

Yes, with one page: in folder mode the Free plan converts one chosen HTML file per run into a self-contained HTML with its local images embedded. Combining multiple HTML files into one file with a shared table of contents requires Pro ($19.99, one-time).

Are my files uploaded anywhere?

No. The folder or ZIP is read in your browser and the combined HTML is created on your device. No site permission is needed, and online images referenced by the pages are left as links rather than downloaded.

Will old Japanese pages turn into garbled text?

Pages without a charset declaration, or declared as UTF-8 but actually written in Shift_JIS, EUC-JP or ISO-2022-JP, are detected automatically and read correctly. If every page has the same <title>, the table of contents uses each page’s heading instead.

What happens to images, PDFs and links between pages?

Local PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP and AVIF images are embedded. Links between the combined pages become jumps inside the file. Linked files such as PDF, Office or ZIP can be copied next to the HTML (default), embedded (20 MiB per file and 100 MiB in total by default) or left as links.

Which files can it read?

.html, .htm and .xhtml files in a folder or a ZIP file. Frame layout pages (frameset) are left out by default while the pages inside the frames are included. MHT files and CHM help files are not read directly; extract a CHM to HTML first.

How is this different from combining pages from a website?

The browser-page mode collects related pages from a live website. Folder mode works only with files already on your computer, so it suits archived sites, offline manuals and documents that are no longer online.

Why is a page or image missing from the result?

Check the report after creating the HTML: it lists files that could not be read, links to pages you left out and images that could not be embedded. Pages outside the chosen sub-folder or in excluded paths are not included, online images stay as links, and frameset layout pages are skipped by default while the pages inside the frames are kept.
